----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------
1. WHAT THE SYSTEM DOES
----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------

In coffeeAGNTCY's Lungo demo, one supervisor agent asks the coffee farms for yields and then
decides alone. The council removes that supervisor. Four stakeholder agents must agree on
how to split one green-coffee order (1,000 lb by default) across the farms listed in a
market directory, and no component decides for them.

  Procurement (Cost)    wants the lowest average price
  Quality (Sommelier)   wants the highest average cup score
  Sustainability        wants low carbon emissions and a high certified share
  Supply Risk           wants on-time delivery and an order spread across origins

These goals conflict. The cheapest farm (Vietnam) has the lowest cup score and the highest
carbon, and it is not certified. The best coffee (Colombia, Ethiopia) costs the most.

How good a plan is for each agent is public: the scoring formulas are shared, so anyone can
score any plan for anyone. What each agent needs is private: the score it currently insists
on (its "bar", which falls each round toward a private floor) and one hard limit it will
veto over. The only way to learn these is to negotiate.

A negotiation ends in one of three ways:

  CONSENSUS             all four agents independently choose and commit to the same plan
  PROCEED_WITH_DISSENT  for a small, reversible order, 3 of 4 go ahead and the fourth
                        agent's objection is recorded
  ESCALATE              the decision goes to a human, with the colliding limits or the best
                        options and who objects to each

The system is pure standard-library Python 3.12+. It needs no API key, network or Docker,
and the same inputs always produce the same transcript. An LLM can optionally put the
agents' messages into words, but it never changes a decision.

2. BIG PICTURE
----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------

           world events (frost, shipment failure, new supplier)
                                     |
                                     v
  Market directory (market.py): farms register, change price, capacity or risk, or drop out
                                     ^
                                     |  agents discover farms and score plans with the
                                     |  same public formulas
       +--------------+--------------+--------------+--------------+
       | Procurement  | Quality      | Sustain-     | Supply Risk  |
       | (Cost)       | (Sommelier)  | ability      |              |
       +--------------+--------------+--------------+--------------+
                                     |
                                     |  publish and subscribe: every agent receives
                                     v  every message
  Shared topic "lungo/council/procurement" (bus.py). Its log is the public record.
  The bus only delivers messages; it never decides.

  The deliberation loop (protocol.py) runs the rounds, injects scheduled events and checks
  the stopping rules against the public record. It never picks the plan.

Every round, each agent:
  1. proposes the plan that is best for itself while closing the gaps the others
     announced, and announces its current bar;
  2. votes on every proposal: APPROVE, REJECT ("I need at least x") or VETO, which reveals
     its hard limit to everyone;
  3. applies the same public selection rule to the round's record by itself and commits to
     the result. Consensus means all four commitments are identical.

----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------
4. THE MARKET MODEL (market.py)
----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------

Farm
  id, price ($/lb), quality (SCA cup score), carbon (kg CO2e per lb), certified (yes/no),
  delay_risk (chance a shipment is late), capacity (the largest share of the order the farm
  can supply) and capabilities (every farm offers "green_coffee").

  farm       price   cup    carbon  certified  delay risk  capacity
  brazil     $3.10   82.0   1.10    no         8%          60%
  colombia   $4.20   86.0   0.90    yes        10%         40%
  vietnam    $2.40   79.0   1.40    no         15%         60%
  honduras   $3.60   84.0   1.00    yes        12%         30%
  ethiopia   $4.60   89.0   0.80    yes        18%         30%   (joins mid-negotiation)

  The first four are listed from the start. Ethiopia registers during a negotiation in the
  frost scenario and in the random trials that include an event.

Market (the shared world state)
  - demand_lb: the order size (1,000 lb by default; 10,000 lb in stubborn_large)
  - a directory: register(farm), discover(capability), which returns only farms with
    capacity left, and update_farm(id, changes)
  - version: goes up on every change, so cached values are recomputed after an event
  - is_supplyable(): can the listed farms still cover the whole order?

Plan
  A dict {farm: share}. Shares sum to 1 and never exceed a farm's capacity.

Scores
  For a plan, each attribute is the share-weighted average: price, cup score, carbon,
  certified share, and on-time probability (1 - delay risk). Each average is normalized to
  0..1 between the worst and best values the currently listed farms can achieve (found by
  filling the best or worst farms first, up to capacity). Scores are therefore relative to
  what the market can offer, and they shift when farms change or join.

    cost_score     normalized price (lower is better)
    quality_score  normalized cup score
    green_score    0.5 x normalized carbon (lower is better)
                   + 0.5 x normalized certified share
    risk_score     0.6 x normalized on-time probability + 0.4 x diversification,
                   where diversification = (1 - HHI) / (1 - 1/n), HHI is the sum of the
                   squared shares and n is the number of listed farms

  describe_plan() prints a plan as, for example,
  "brazil 42%, colombia 23%, honduras 22%, vietnam 13%  | $3.38/lb, cup 83.0, ...".


----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------
5. THE AGENTS (agents.py)
----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------

Each StakeholderAgent holds:

  public     id, title, role (which scoring function it maximizes)
  private    floor               the lowest score it will ever accept
             concession          how much its bar drops per round
             constraint          the one hard limit it vetoes over
  learned    others_bars         the bar each other agent last announced
             known_constraints   hard limits revealed by vetoes or updates
  internal   patience            rounds of conceding so far (halved by new information)
  switch     listens             False in the ablation: ignore the bars others announce

The default council (scenarios override some of these values):

  agent     title                 hard limit (private)               floor  concession
  cost      Procurement (Cost)    average price <= $3.70/lb          0.45   0.06
  quality   Quality (Sommelier)   average cup score >= 83.0          0.40   0.05
  green     Sustainability        certified share >= 35%             0.45   0.07
  risk      Supply Risk           no origin above 50% of the order   0.50   0.05

Hard limits (HardConstraint)
  Four kinds: max_price, min_quality, min_certified and max_share. violation(plan) measures
  how far a plan breaks the limit (0 means respected). describe() gives the sentence used in
  veto messages, such as "average cup score must be at least 83.0".

The bar
  bar = max(floor, 1 - concession x patience). It starts at 1.0, so at first only the
  agent's ideal plan is good enough, and it falls every round until it reaches the floor.

propose()
  Each agent searches (with the solver, section 9) for the plan that maximizes

      its own score
    - accommodation x (total shortfall below the bars the other agents announced)
    - 50 x (violation of every hard limit it knows: its own and those revealed)

  where accommodation = (0.5 + 0.5 x patience) / (number of other agents). Agents open by
  arguing for themselves and give the others' needs more weight as talks go on. Dividing by
  the number of others stops a crowd of demands from drowning out the agent's own goal.
  Known hard limits are never traded away.

vote(plan), on every proposal including its own
  VETO      the plan breaks the agent's hard limit; the vote carries the limit, revealing it
  APPROVE   the agent's score for the plan is at least its current bar
  REJECT    anything else; the vote states the score and the need, e.g. "0.31 < needs 0.65"

observe(message)
  Runs for every message on the topic, except the agent's own. It records the bars
  announced in PROPOSALs and the limits revealed by VETO votes and CONSTRAINT_UPDATEs. This
  is the only way agents learn about each other.

on_event(briefing)
  New information halves patience, so bars rise again and the negotiation reopens. A private
  briefing can change the agent's floor or hard limit. If the others already know the old
  limit, or the briefing says to announce it, the agent publishes a CONSTRAINT_UPDATE.


----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------
6. MESSAGING (bus.py)
----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------

All agents publish to and subscribe to one topic, "lungo/council/procurement". Every
subscriber receives every message, in order. The bus appends each message to a log, which
is the public record. It only delivers messages and never decides anything.

A Message has a round, sender, kind, payload (structured data) and text (human-readable).

  kind               sender     payload
  EVENT              world      title, farm updates, new farms
  CONSTRAINT_UPDATE  an agent   its changed hard limit
  PROPOSAL           an agent   plan, announced bar, whether it is at its floor
  VOTE               an agent   the proposal, then APPROVE with the score, REJECT with the
                                score and need, or VETO with the hard limit and its sentence
  COMMIT             an agent   hash of the plan it chose
  STALEMATE          all        everyone is at their floor with no unanimous plan
  DECISION           all        final status and plan

InProcessBus has the same publish/subscribe shape as the SLIM/NATS group messaging in
AGNTCY's app-sdk, so agents only ever talk through a topic interface.


----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------
7. THE DELIBERATION PROTOCOL (protocol.py)
----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------

Deliberation(market, agents, events, max_rounds=25, supermajority=3,
             auto_approve_limit_usd=5000)

runs rounds until a stopping rule fires. Transcripts label rounds r0, r1, ...; this
document counts them from 1, so "round 1" is r0. In this in-process build, one loop drives
the turn order for all four agents; in a distributed deployment each agent would run the
same steps itself.

Each round:
  1. New information: if an event is scheduled for this round, apply it (section 8).
  2. Supply check: if the listed farms can no longer cover the order, ESCALATE.
  3. Proposals: every agent proposes a plan and announces its bar and whether it is at its
     floor.
  4. Votes: every agent votes on every proposal.
  5. Local decisions: every agent applies the same public rule to the round's record.
     Among the plans all four approved, take the one with the largest minimum margin (each
     agent's score minus its announced bar), breaking ties by proposer id. Bars are compared
     as announced (rounded), so all agents compute identical margins. Each agent publishes a
     COMMIT with a hash of its choice (SHA-256 of the rounded plan). If all four hashes
     match, the outcome is CONSENSUS.
  6. Stopping checks, using only public information:
     - If the hard limits revealed so far cannot all hold at once (tested with the solver),
       ESCALATE immediately. The outcome names the minimal colliding limits (those whose
       removal alone would make the rest achievable) and lists the current options.
     - If every agent has announced it is at its floor for two rounds in a row, it is a
       stalemate (below).
  7. Every agent's patience goes up by one, and the next round starts.

If the round limit is reached, the stalemate rules apply.

Stalemate
  Rank this round's proposals by how many agents approved them, ignoring any that someone
  vetoed. If the best remaining plan has at least 3 of 4 approvals and the order is worth
  $5,000 or less (average price x pounds), the council goes ahead: PROCEED_WITH_DISSENT,
  with each dissenter's objection recorded, such as "cost: 0.307 < needs 0.45". Otherwise it
  ESCALATEs with the options: each proposal, who approved it and how the others objected.

Stopping rules at a glance

  situation                                                   outcome
  all four approve a plan and commit the same hash            CONSENSUS
  the revealed hard limits cannot all be met                  ESCALATE at once, naming them
  all at their floors for 2 rounds, 3 of 4 back a veto-free   PROCEED_WITH_DISSENT
    plan, and the order is worth $5,000 or less
  the same stalemate on a bigger order, or no 3-of-4 plan     ESCALATE with the options
  the farms can no longer cover the order                     ESCALATE (re-scope the order)
  the round limit is reached                                  handled as a stalemate

The result is an Outcome: status, reason, plan, rounds, the full message log, options for
a human, recorded dissent and the conflicting limits.


----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------
8. NEW INFORMATION (EVENTS)
----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------

An Event has a round, a title, farm updates, new farms and private briefings for individual
agents. When it fires:
  - the market changes, so every score is renormalized against the new market;
  - an EVENT message announces it ("NEW INFORMATION: ...");
  - each agent runs on_event with its briefing, raising its bar again;
  - any changed hard limits are announced with CONSTRAINT_UPDATE;
  - the stalemate counter resets.

Example: the frost event, at the start of round 4 (r3)
  - Brazil can now supply only 20% of the order, at $3.60/lb with a 30% delay risk.
  - Ethiopia registers in the directory (certified, cup 89, $4.60/lb).
  - Private briefings: Supply Risk tightens its limit to 40% per origin and announces it;
    Procurement's budget ceiling rises to $3.95/lb.

run.py measures the effect of the news. It also runs the same scenario without the event
and judges that "stale" deal against the world after the event. In frost, the stale deal
leaves 22% of the order undeliverable and breaks Supply Risk's new limit, while the
renegotiated deal satisfies all four agents.


----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------
9. THE PLANNER (solver.py)
----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------

maximize(market, objective) is a deterministic local search with no outside dependencies.
It starts from five plans: shares proportional to capacity, plus greedy fills by lowest
price, highest cup score, lowest carbon and lowest delay risk. From each start it keeps
moving share from one farm to another, always taking the best improving move, with step
sizes of 0.20, 0.10, 0.05, 0.02, 0.01 and 0.005. Every intermediate plan respects
capacities and sums to 1. The best result is returned, rounded to 4 decimals.

It is used for every agent's proposals, the feasibility check and all the baselines.


----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------
10. BASELINES AND THE SCORECARD (baselines.py)
----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------

Simpler ways to decide, run on the same world for comparison:

  weighted_sum    A central optimizer that maximizes the average of the four public scores.
                  It cannot see anyone's private floor or hard limit.
  majority_vote   One shot. Each agent submits its own best plan (respecting only its own
                  limit), each agent approves the plans that meet its limit and floor, and
                  the plan with the most approvals wins.
  oracle          A central planner that knows every private floor and limit and maximizes
                  the worst-off agent's margin. An upper bound for comparison, not a
                  realistic competitor.

scorecard(plan) judges any plan against everyone's true private preferences on the final
market: each agent's score, the minimum and mean, which hard limits it breaks, which agents
are truly satisfied (limit respected and score at or above the floor), and how much of the
order is undeliverable (shares above a farm's current capacity).

Ground-truth checks: zopa_exists (can the oracle find a plan that satisfies all four?) and
hard_feasible (can all four hard limits hold at once?).


----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------
11. SCENARIOS (scenarios.py)
----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------

A Scenario has a name, a story, a build() function that returns a fresh market and fresh
agents (with any overrides), optional events, settings (order size, round limit) and the
expected outcome. The expected outcome is only compared after a run, by run.py and the
tests. Agents never see it.

  scenario        difficulty                    result
  quick_deal      easy                          CONSENSUS after 3 rounds
  harvest         in between                    CONSENSUS after 13 rounds
  frost           in between, new information   CONSENSUS after 11 rounds
  hard_line       long                          CONSENSUS after 37 rounds
  budget_clash    impossible                    ESCALATE after 1 round
  stubborn_small  impossible                    PROCEED_WITH_DISSENT after 14 rounds
  stubborn_large  impossible                    ESCALATE after 14 rounds

  quick_deal      Harvest's limits, but every agent's bar drops 0.35 a round. Once the
                  vetoes in round 1 reveal the hard limits, they agree in round 3, on the
                  same plan as harvest.
  harvest         The default council with Procurement's floor at 0.25. Procurement opens
                  with 60% Vietnam ($2.68/lb); Quality, Sustainability and Supply Risk veto
                  it, revealing their limits. Bars fall until all four approve: Brazil 42% /
                  Colombia 23% / Honduras 22% / Vietnam 13% at $3.38/lb.
  frost           Harvest plus the frost event (section 8). The agents renegotiate around
                  the new farm: Vietnam 35% / Ethiopia 30% / Honduras 27% / Colombia 8% at
                  $3.52/lb.
  hard_line       Procurement's ceiling is $3.50/lb, Sustainability needs at least 50%
                  certified, Supply Risk allows at most 40% per origin, and every bar drops
                  only 0.02 a round (the round limit is raised to 60). All four veto in
                  round 1. Final plan: Brazil 30% / Colombia 30% / Vietnam 20% / Honduras
                  20% at $3.39/lb. On this world the weighted-sum optimizer and the
                  majority vote both break Procurement's $3.50 limit.
  budget_clash    Procurement's ceiling is $3.25/lb and Quality needs cup 84. No plan meets
                  both, so the council escalates after round 1, naming exactly those two
                  limits.
  stubborn_small  The default council: Procurement's floor of 0.45 cannot be reached within
                  the others' limits. The 1,000 lb order (about $3,400) is under $5,000, so
                  the other three go ahead and record "cost: 0.307 < needs 0.45".
  stubborn_large  The same disagreement on a 10,000 lb order (about $34,000). That is too
                  big to override a stakeholder, so the council escalates with three
                  options and the objections to each.

Across quick_deal, harvest and hard_line, how fast agents give ground changes how long the
talks take; quick_deal and harvest end on the same plan.


----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------
12. RUNNING SCENARIOS (run.py)
----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------

run_scenario(scenario):
  1. builds the world and runs the Deliberation;
  2. builds a fresh copy brought to the post-event state, then uses the scorecard to judge
     the council's plan and the three baselines' plans on it;
  3. records the ground truth (zopa_exists, hard_feasible);
  4. for scenarios with events, also runs the no-event version and judges that stale plan
     on the post-event world.

Command line: python3 -m council.run <scenario|all> [--verbose] [--llm] [--json FILE]

It prints a condensed transcript (events, limit updates, vetoes, the proposals in rounds 1
and 2, and the decision; --verbose prints every message), the outcome, and a comparison
table showing, for the council and each baseline, how many agents are satisfied, how many
limits are broken, and the minimum and mean scores. It exits with an error code if any
outcome differs from the expected one.

results/scenarios.log and results/scenarios.json are the saved output of "run all".

13. RANDOMIZED EVALUATION (evaluate.py)
----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------

python3 -m council.evaluate --trials 300 --json results/evaluation.json

Each trial builds a random world from a seed (seed x 1000 + trial number; the default seed
is 7):
  - farms: price and carbon x 0.85 to 1.15, cup score +/- 2 points, delay risk x 0.7 to
    1.4 (capped at 50%)
  - each agent: floor 0.15 to 0.55, concession 0.03 to 0.10 per round, and a random hard
    limit (price ceiling $3.20 to $4.00, cup minimum 81.5 to 84.5, certified minimum 20% to
    60%, origin maximum 35% to 60%)
  - one trial in three: in round 3, 4 or 5, a shipment failure cuts Brazil, Vietnam or
    Colombia to 0-20% capacity with a 35% delay risk, Ethiopia joins, and Supply Risk
    tightens to 40% per origin

Every world runs twice: normally, and as an ablation in which agents ignore the bars the
others announce. Plans are judged against the agents' true private preferences.

Saved results for 300 worlds. Ground truth: a plan all four would accept exists in 181
worlds; in 50 the hard limits are compatible but the floors are not; in 69 the hard limits
collide.

  method                            decided   all satisfied  broke a limit  mean min score
  council                           209/300   171            0              0.40
  weighted-sum optimizer            300/300   2              244            0.06
  one-shot majority vote            300/300   21             219            0.05
  full-information oracle           300/300   181            74             0.37
  ablation: ignore announced bars   209/300   133            0              0.36

  - The council reached consensus in 93.9% of the worlds where agreement was possible
    (170 of 181) and escalated in 100% of the worlds whose hard limits collide. It never
    adopted a plan that broke a hard limit; the 91 worlds it did not decide went to a human.
  - New information: in 61 event trials, deals reached before the event left 13.3% of the
    order undeliverable on average and broke a hard limit in 26. The council's renegotiated
    plans left nothing undeliverable and broke no limits.
  - Listening matters: when agents ignore the announced bars, consensus in the worlds where
    agreement was possible drops from 93.9% to 73.5%, and talks average 10.9 rounds instead
    of 9.4.


----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------
14. OPTIONAL LLM NARRATION (narrate.py)
----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------

  LLM_MODEL=<model> python3 -m council.run frost --llm

LLM_MODEL is any litellm model name, such as an Anthropic model (with an API key) or a
local Ollama model like ollama_chat/llama3.1:8b. It needs the litellm package, the only
dependency outside the standard library.

Each agent gets a persona: a blunt procurement lead, a meticulous sommelier, a
sustainability officer, a supply-chain risk manager. The LLM rewrites only the text of
PROPOSAL and VOTE messages, in one or two first-person sentences that keep every number.
Plans, votes, bars and decisions are computed by the agents' code, so the outcome is
identical with or without narration. If a call fails, the original text is kept and marked
"(narration unavailable: ...)".

Principle: the LLM narrates, the code decides. That keeps runs reproducible and prevents an
LLM from talking the agents into an agreement their real preferences don't support.


----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------
15. AGNTCY INTEGRATION (cards.py)
----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------

python3 -m council.cards writes one A2A AgentCard per agent to cards/council_<id>.json, in
the same shape as Lungo's farm cards:
  - a name and description, such as "Council Quality (Sommelier)"
  - one skill, "deliberate", tagged coffee, procurement, negotiation and objective:<role>
  - SLIM as the preferred transport, at slim://<SLIM_SERVER>/lungo/agents/council_<id>,
    plus a NATS interface on the shared topic (the SLIM_SERVER and NATS_SERVER environment
    variables default to slim:46357 and nats:4222)

A coffee exchange could discover the council agents through the AGNTCY directory the same
way Lungo discovers farms.

Not done yet: running each agent as a separate AgntcyFactory service on Lungo's
docker-compose stack (Docker was unavailable when this was built). The agents already
communicate only through the topic interface, but the deliberation loop currently calls
each agent in turn inside one process. A distributed version would give each agent its own
loop that reacts to the round's messages.

16. TESTS (tests/test_council.py)
----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------

python3 -m unittest discover -s tests -t .     (11 tests, a few seconds)

Scenario tests
  - every scenario reaches its expected outcome
  - runs are deterministic: running frost twice gives an identical transcript
  - harvest's consensus truly satisfies all four agents and breaks no limit
  - stronger positions take longer (quick_deal, then harvest, then hard_line), and all three
    deals still satisfy all four agents

    limit, and the council does not

  - budget_clash names exactly Procurement's and Quality's limits for the human
  - stubborn_small records the dissent and breaks no limit

Protocol tests
  - private limits start unknown and are revealed only by vetoes
  - all commitment hashes match on consensus
  - the scorecard flags broken limits


----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------
17. KEY DESIGN DECISIONS
----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------

No component chooses the plan.
  Each agent applies the selection rule itself. Consensus means four independent choices
  produce the same hash.

Public scores, private needs.
  Anyone can evaluate a plan for anyone, but how much each agent needs and what it will
  never accept are private. That makes negotiation necessary, and it gives the messages
  real information to carry.

A veto explains itself.
  "No, because the cup score must be at least 83" turns a rejection into a limit everyone
  can plan around. A bare "no" only leads to more counter-offers.

Accommodation grows with time.
  When agents gave every announced bar full weight from round 1, Procurement proposed a plan
  that scored 0 for itself. Scaling accommodation by time and by the number of other agents
  keeps each agent arguing for its own goal.

Different disagreements get different exits.
  Colliding hard limits are detected at once and go to a human. Floors that can't all be
  met only show up after the concessions run out; they can be acted on with recorded dissent
  when the stakes are small and reversible.

Determinism.
  Every run replays byte-for-byte, which makes bugs traceable. One apparent false consensus
  turned out to be a rounding bug in the scorecard, not a flaw in the agents.

The LLM narrates; the code decides.
  Language is optional decoration. Decisions come from the agents' logic.


----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------
18. HOW THE DESIGN MEETS THE PROJECT BRIEF
----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------

At least three autonomous agents with distinct objectives
  -> Four agents, each with its own scoring function, private floor, concession rate and
     hard limit.

A real conflict, not scripted agreement
  -> The objectives conflict by construction (cheap coffee is lower quality, uncertified
     and higher carbon). Outcomes depend on the agents' private settings and their
     exchanges: 91 of 300 random worlds end in escalation, and agents that ignore each
     other's announced needs reach consensus far less often (73.5% vs 93.9%).

Agents exchange evidence, challenge proposals and revise positions
  -> Every round brings new proposals and a vote on each one. A REJECT states what the
     agent needs, a VETO states the limit behind it, and every agent plans around what it
     hears.

The effect of new information
  -> Events change the market and give agents private briefings. Agents raise their bars,
     announce new limits and renegotiate (the frost scenario and 61 random event trials).

No supervisor imposes the final decision
  -> Each agent decides locally and commits. The loop only runs the rounds and checks
     stopping rules that use public information.

Compare with an individual-agent or simpler baseline
  -> A weighted-sum central optimizer, a one-shot majority vote, a full-information
     oracle, and a no-listening ablation.

Know when to stop, preserve disagreement or seek human input
  -> ESCALATE for colliding limits, stalemates on large orders and supply failures;
     PROCEED_WITH_DISSENT with the objection on record for small, reversible orders.

Can independent agents make better decisions together than individually?
  -> In 300 random worlds, the council satisfied all four agents in 171, compared with 21
     for a majority vote and 2 for a central optimizer, and it never adopted a plan that
     broke a hard limit.


----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------
19. LIMITATIONS AND NEXT STEPS
----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------

- About 6% of agreement zones are missed. Every planner is a local search, and announced
  bars lag one round behind. A better planner (an LP, or proposing the max-min plan near the
  end) should close part of the gap.
- The oracle and the ground-truth checks use the same local search, so "agreement exists"
  is approximate. In one random world, the council found an agreement the oracle missed.
- Agents report their bars honestly. A strategic agent could inflate its bar; a natural
  extension is to cap claims at what the agent could credibly get elsewhere.
- The bus runs in one process, and one loop drives all four agents in turn. Running them as
  separate AGNTCY services is the remaining integration step.
- Agents negotiate only how to split the order: there are no side payments, no price
  negotiation with farms and no trades across several decisions.
